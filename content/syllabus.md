## Approved Course Description

### Catalog Description

> This course explores fundamental programming concepts and provides
> students with the tools they need to apply programming to their own
> creative practice. It examines ways in which coding can be used
> artistically to create something expressive rather than only
> functional. Student will investigate how they can utilize programming
> to open new opportunities and adapt to changing technologies.

### Additional Course Information

This section is conducted in a flexible, research-oriented format. The
cohort will meet weekly and work toward a laptop-ensemble performance
centered on live coding for music, sound, visuals, or combinations of
these. Students will compare tools, conduct short creative experiments,
document their process, and develop a collaborative performance. A
student for whom the ensemble model is not a good fit may propose an
independent creative-coding project; its research question, scope,
milestones, documentation, and final outcome must be approved by the
instructor.

## Prerequisites

### Course Prerequisite(s)

CSC 150.

### Technology Skills

- Basic programming skills, including variables, conditionals, loops,
  functions, and debugging.

- Ability to install or access course-approved software, manage project
  files, and use D2L.

- No previous experience with live coding, electronic music, sound
  synthesis, or audiovisual performance is required.

## Student Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Explain how artists use code as an expressive medium and how live
  coding makes programming part of a performance.

- Create and revise music, sound, visual, or audiovisual studies in one
  or more creative-coding environments.

- Frame a creative question, conduct iterative experiments, document
  evidence, and reflect on technical and aesthetic results.

- Read, debug, adapt, and explain the code used in their own creative
  work.

- Collaborate in an ensemble by developing a role, responding to other
  performers, and contributing reliably to rehearsals and shared
  outcomes.

- Present, document, and critically evaluate a completed creative-coding
  project or live performance.

## Course Materials

### Required Textbook(s)

No textbook is required. Readings, documentation, tutorials, recordings,
and viewing examples will be provided through D2L.

### Required Supplementary Materials

A laptop capable of running a current web browser and course-approved
creative-coding software; reliable internet access; and wired
headphones. The class will select tools according to student interests
and project needs. Options include Strudel, Sonic Pi, Gibber, Hydra,
SuperCollider, and TidalCycles. Browser-based options will be used when
practical.

### Optional Materials

MIDI controllers, audio interfaces, microphones, cameras, projectors, or
other sensors and performance devices may be used when appropriate.

## Course Delivery and Instructional Methods

This course uses a flexible, research-oriented studio model rather than
a conventional lecture schedule:

- One weekly in-person cohort meeting for demonstrations, experiments,
  rehearsals, critique, and planning.

- Individual or small-group research conferences scheduled as projects
  require.

- Independent technical and creative practice documented through code,
  recordings, screen captures, notes, and reflections.

- Collaborative laptop-ensemble development, with an approved
  independent-project pathway when appropriate.

- D2L for announcements, materials, milestone submissions, feedback, and
  the official grade record.

## Evaluation Procedures

### Assessments

> The final grade reflects sustained research practice, technical and
> creative growth, collaboration, documentation, and the completed
> performance or independent project.

- Weekly research practice and participation — 25%

  - Preparation, attendance, experiments, progress notes, critique, and
    evidence of consistent iteration.

- Creative-coding studies — 30%

  - A sequence of short music/sound, visual, audiovisual, or interaction
    studies developed in selected environments.

- Project proposal and milestones — 20%

  - Research question, tool and role selection, annotated sketches,
    work-in-progress demonstrations, rehearsal contributions, and
    revision plan.

- Final performance or independent project — 25%

  - Completed creative work, public or class presentation, reproducible
    code/project files, documentation, and a concise critical
    reflection.

Detailed criteria and milestone dates will be posted in D2L. The D2L
gradebook is the source of truth for recorded grades.

### Final Examination

The final laptop-ensemble showcase and project review serve as the final
examination. Students completing an independent project will present

arrangement is announced in D2L. Check D2L for the location. Attendance and presentation are required
unless an alternative is approved in advance.

## Classroom Policies

### Attendance and Make-up Policy

Because this is a small research cohort and performance ensemble, the
weekly group meeting, scheduled critiques, rehearsals, and individual
conferences are central course work. Students should notify the
instructor by email before an absence whenever possible and should
continue documenting progress between meetings.

When an absence is excused under university policy or an alternative is
approved, the instructor will identify an equivalent progress check or
make-up activity when the collaborative work can reasonably be
reproduced. A missed rehearsal, critique, or performance may not be
fully replaceable because classmates depend on each member's
contribution.

Every individual submission has an automatic 72-hour grace period unless
it is tied to a live critique, rehearsal, performance, or shared
technical test. No request is needed during the grace period. After it
closes, work is accepted only for a documented excused absence, an
approved accommodation, or another arrangement made with the instructor.

### AI Use Policy

AI-assisted tools may be used for brainstorming, explaining unfamiliar
code, debugging, comparing approaches, or generating small experiments
when their use supports learning. Students must disclose the tool and
describe how it was used in the research log or submission notes; copied
or generated code must be tested, understood, and adapted by the
student.

AI may not replace the student's creative decision-making, research
documentation, critical reflection, or ability to explain and perform
the submitted system. Do not submit opaque generated code, fabricate
sources or process evidence, or use a system in ways that violate
copyright, privacy, license terms, or course collaboration agreements.
Assignment-specific directions may narrow or expand these uses.

secure D2L login, in-person meetings and conferences, iterative project
evidence, rehearsals, and the final presentation.
